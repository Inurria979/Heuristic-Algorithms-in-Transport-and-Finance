# The PJS savings heuristic for the Team Orienteering Problem (Chao instances)

Lab activity on the Team Orienteering Problem (TOP): `m` vehicles leave an origin, visit
customers that each carry a reward and must reach a destination within a time limit
`tmax`; the goal is to maximise the total reward collected. We implement the **PJS
heuristic** of Panadero et al. (2021) — Clarke & Wright savings enriched with the rewards —,
combine it with **GRASP** (biased-randomised multi-start) and add a **local search** on
the elite solutions, and test everything on the 387 instances of Chao et al. (1996).

Unlike the other projects, the whole pipeline is written in **Rust** with
[rayon](https://github.com/rayon-rs/rayon): experiments, figures (plotters) and the
Markdown reports. There is no Python environment in this folder.

## Usage

Requires [rustup](https://rustup.rs/); `src/rust/rust-toolchain.toml` selects the stable GNU
toolchain. Always build from `src/rust`, so that `.cargo/config.toml` sends the binaries to
`ignore/target` (single release profile: fat LTO, one codegen unit, `target-cpu=native`):

```bash
cd src/rust
cargo build --release
cargo test --release
```

Main experiment (α grid, GRASP and local search, about 2 min), from `TOP/`:

```bash
ignore/target/release/pjs.exe grid --png-dir results/figures/routes --report docs/informe_alpha.md \
    --ls-png-dir results/figures/routes_ls --ls-report docs/informe_busqueda_local.md data/top-data results
```

The comparisons with Panadero et al. (`pjs panadero`, `pjs bks`) and the route check
(`verify_routes`) are documented, with their exact commands, in
[`docs/scratchpad.md`](docs/scratchpad.md).

## Method in short

* **PJS.** Start from one route per customer, compute the enriched saving
  `s'_ij = α s_ij + (1 − α)(u_i + u_j)` for every arc in **both directions** (origin and
  destination differ, so `s_ij ≠ s_ji`), merge routes along the sorted list whenever the
  result fits within `tmax`, and keep the `m` routes with the highest reward.
* **α grid.** The paper gives no value of α, and its qualitative rule does not hold on the
  Chao instances, so every instance is solved with 21 values (0, 0.05, …, 1) and the best
  one is kept.
* **GRASP.** For each instance and α, 10 runs of 10 iterations with a geometric selector
  (β = 0.3, as in the paper) and 10 with a softmax selector (τ = 1, our proposal); each run
  starts from the deterministic solution and keeps the best construction. Seeds are derived
  per (instance, α, selector, run), so results do not depend on the thread schedule.
* **Local search** on the elite solutions (the 3 best of each group, duplicates removed):
  2-opt inside each route plus insertion of the most rewarding unvisited customer that fits,
  repeated while a customer can be inserted.
* **Scale.** 387 × 21 × 21 = 170 667 independent tasks in one rayon parallel loop.

## Results (`pjs grid` + `pjs bks`)

Total reward over the 353 feasible instances ([`results/best_solution.csv`](results/best_solution.csv)):
deterministic PJS with the best α **157 868**, GRASP **170 469** (+7.98 %), GRASP + local
search **189 854** (+20.26 %).

Mean gap (%) to the best known solution per set ([`results/bks_by_set.csv`](results/bks_by_set.csv)):

| Set | Instances | Deterministic | GRASP | GRASP + local search | Panadero et al., 128 threads | BKS reached (final) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| p1 | 48 | 6.88 | 3.15 | 0.58 | 0.69 | 40 |
| p2 | 33 | 1.81 | 0.31 | 0.00 | 0.11 | 33 |
| p3 | 60 | 6.84 | 2.96 | 0.67 | 1.44 | 48 |
| p4 | 56 | 29.25 | 23.73 | 5.87 | 7.97 | 5 |
| p5 | 74 | 11.82 | 4.49 | 1.30 | 1.46 | 35 |
| p6 | 24 | 14.45 | 8.51 | 1.17 | 1.42 | 14 |
| p7 | 58 | 19.75 | 14.48 | 3.42 | 5.10 | 14 |
| **All** | **353** | **13.61** | **8.63** | **2.04** | **2.85** | **189** |

Our gap is `100 (BKS − reward) / BKS`. The Panadero column is read from Figure 4 of their
paper (the *All* row weights it by the number of instances), and in their tables they divide
by their own reward instead of the BKS; with that denominator our overall gap would be
2.17 % instead of 2.04 %. No final solution exceeds the BKS. The reward gained over the
deterministic PJS is largest in the two biggest sets, p4 (+38.75 %) and p7 (+28.07 %), and
smallest in p2 (+2.09 %). The computational effort is not the same as
Panadero's, so the comparison is indicative; see the article and
[`docs/metodos_y_comparacion_panadero.md`](docs/metodos_y_comparacion_panadero.md).
